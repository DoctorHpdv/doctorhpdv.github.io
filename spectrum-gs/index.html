Abstract

3D Gaussian Splatting has gained increasing attention in remote sensing and photogrammetry for its efficient rendering and flexible scene representation. However, reconstructing city-scale scenes across extreme observation-scale ranges remains challenging: building boundaries, repetitive textures, and thin structures can become blurred or incomplete as their projected size changes substantially between views.

We propose Spectrum-GS, a continuous scale-adaptive framework that connects Gaussian rendering and density optimization through a shared observation-scale condition. Structure from Motion geometry provides coarse scale guidance, which is refined using global viewing information and local scene cues. Matching the resulting condition to learnable Gaussian scale receptive fields drives the adaptation of opacity, spatial extent, and color. Experiments on BungeeNeRF, Mill19, UrbanScene3D, and MatrixCity demonstrate improved rendering quality over the Momentum-GS backbone.
